# Camera and lens looks for static images

Camera bodies and cinema lenses originate in motion-picture production, but their visible rendering characteristics can guide a static image. Use them as capture-language references, not as empty prestige labels.

## 1. Compile visible rendering before equipment names

Build the clause in this order:

1. focal behaviour and perspective
2. subject/background separation and focus falloff
3. contrast and micro-contrast
4. skin and colour rendering
5. highlight roll-off and shadow separation
6. bokeh shape and edge behaviour
7. flare, halation, aberration, grain, or softness
8. optional `Shot on ...` or `... lens character` label

Example:

`Natural warm skin rendering, smooth focus falloff, rounded bokeh, organic midtone contrast, controlled highlight roll-off, and restrained edge softness, with a Cooke S4/i lens character on an ARRI ALEXA 35-inspired digital cinema image.`

Do not expect a model name alone to determine the look. Never include frame rate, codec, recording format, camera weight, metadata, mount, gearing, or rental-market details; they are not visible in a still.

## 2. Choose focal behaviour

- wide-angle look: greater foreground/background scale difference, stronger spatial depth, broader setting coverage, possible edge stretching
- natural-perspective look: restrained distortion and familiar facial proportions
- long-lens / telephoto look: compressed depth, isolated subject, layered background appearing closer
- macro look: magnified surface detail and a very thin focus plane
- spherical cinema look: rounder bokeh, conventional perspective, controlled geometry
- anamorphic look: wider spatial impression, elliptical bokeh, restrained horizontal flare, mild edge deformation

Specify approximate focal length only when it helps: wide 20–35mm, natural 40–58mm, portrait 75–105mm, compressed telephoto 135mm and above. Match the stated focal length to the perspective and camera distance.

## 3. Camera-body rendering references

Use one body at most, and only if it adds useful direction.

| ARRI ALEXA Mini | Neutral cinema colour, organic contrast, dependable highlight handling, compact observational or action-documentary feeling. |

| Sony VENICE 2 | Clean full-frame rendering, refined low-light colour separation, controlled highlights, modern polish. |

## 4. Cooke rendering families

`Cooke Look` does not mean simply soft. Its useful static-image traits are smooth dimensional rendering, natural-to-warm skin, organic contrast, graceful focus falloff, pleasing bokeh, and clarity without brittle edges.

| S8/i FF | Modern full-frame polish with Cooke personality | High resolving detail without brittle sharpness, smooth spherical bokeh, minimal colour fringing, contrast tuned for digital capture, refined warm skin. |
| S7/i FF | Balanced full-frame narrative work | Consistent colour, dimensional faces, smooth tonal separation, warm natural skin, controlled contrast. |
| S4/i | Classic modern Cooke character | Organic clarity, warm skin, smooth focus transition, gentle dimensionality, controlled flare and distortion. Do not mislabel it as a deliberately soft vintage lens. |
| MiniS4/i | S4/i-like character for an intimate or mobile-production feeling | Organic clean rendering, natural skin, smooth bokeh, restrained contrast; the physical lightness of the lens is irrelevant to a still image. |

| Speed Panchro / SP3 inspired | Older Cooke personality without uncontrolled degradation | Warm organic colour, gentle focus falloff, mild vintage softness, subtle edge looseness, painterly dimensionality. |

## 5. Other modern cinema-lens directions

- ARRI/ZEISS Master Prime inspired: highly controlled geometry, high micro-contrast, reduced flare, crisp edges, neutral and predictable rendering; useful for VFX, architecture, product, or precise commercial work.
- Leitz Summilux-C inspired: warm skin, gentle focus falloff, clean flat-field consistency, refined clarity; useful when lighting and production design should carry most of the style.
- Angénieux Optimo inspired: controlled distortion and chromatic aberration, polished colour, clean zoom-lens consistency; use visible traits rather than mentioning zoom movement in a still.
- modern neutral cinema prime: clean contrast, controlled flare, consistent edge detail, restrained aberration, natural colour.

Choose Cooke when the lens should participate visibly in emotional rendering; choose a more neutral controlled direction when geometry, compositing, products, or production design need a clean canvas.

## 8. Match optical character to scene structure

- Swirl requires a centred subject and a textured or point-highlight background. It weakens on plain walls.
- Bubble bokeh requires bright, discrete out-of-focus highlights; do not request it in a flat, evenly lit background.
- Anamorphic flare needs a visible hard light source or strong specular highlight aligned to create it.
- Large-format separation still requires plausible camera distance and focus depth; do not demand full-scene sharpness and extreme shallow depth simultaneously.
- Telephoto compression requires a distant camera position and layered background, not merely the word `telephoto`.
- Wide-angle spatial drama requires a relatively close foreground element and visible depth layers.
- Vintage low contrast and modern high micro-contrast conflict. Choose a priority or describe a controlled hybrid explicitly.
- Strong optical signatures can distract from exact product geometry, typography, multi-person identity, and reference fidelity. Prefer neutral optics for those tasks unless the user requests otherwise.

## 9. Selection guide

- natural narrative portrait: ARRI ALEXA 35-inspired rendering + Cooke S4/i or S7/i character
- refined modern commercial portrait: Sony VENICE 2-inspired rendering + S8/i or neutral modern prime
- period memory or romantic scene: Panchro/i Classic, Speed Panchro, Primoplan, or Jupiter-9 character
- visibly eccentric portrait: Biotar or Helios swirl; Trioplan bubble bokeh when background highlights support it
- environmental portrait: Flektogon or Mir wide vintage character; use controlled edge distortion
- exact product, UI, or typography: neutral modern prime or Master Prime-inspired control; avoid swirl, bubble bokeh, aggressive flare, and strong edge falloff
- high-end large-format prestige still: ALEXA LF / DXL2-inspired rendering + Panchro 65/i or clean large-format prime character
- surveillance, documentary, or raw realism: Blackmagic-style practical texture or neutral camera rendering; avoid prestige lens language unless requested

## 10. Prompt clause patterns

### Modern Cooke portrait

`A natural-perspective medium close-up with warm, lifelike skin rendering, smooth focus falloff, rounded bokeh, organic midtone contrast, dimensional facial modelling, and controlled highlight roll-off, using an ARRI ALEXA 35-inspired digital cinema response with Cooke S4/i lens character.`

### Painterly vintage memory

`A gently compressed portrait with painterly central detail, warm organic colour, subtle wide-open glow, natural edge falloff, smooth focus transition, mild flare susceptibility, and restrained vintage softness, inspired by Cooke Panchro/i Classic rendering.`

### Helios-style character portrait

`A centred portrait with the face held sharply near the optical centre while a textured, point-lit background dissolves into pronounced swirled peripheral bokeh; lower contrast, gentle veiling flare, stretched edge blur, and a Helios-44-2 58mm f/2 character.`

### Clean product image

`Neutral colour, high geometric control, crisp but non-brittle surface detail, minimal distortion, restrained flare, consistent edge definition, controlled specular highlights, and a modern cinema-prime character suitable for exact product form.`

Use these as patterns, not boilerplate. Keep only the traits that support the actual image.
